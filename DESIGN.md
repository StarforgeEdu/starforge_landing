# Design System: Starforge

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Working Courtyard"**

Starforge feels like a contemporary shared courtyard: warm, calm, and unmistakably regional, with every role opening onto one ordered center. The identity combines Central Asian material warmth with the precision of an operational product. It should feel human enough for education and rigorous enough for finance, permissions, and leadership decisions.

The system is editorial without becoming ornamental. Large modern sans-serif statements carry conviction; compact code-native interfaces provide proof. Geometry is structural, star-derived, and used at decisive scale. The finished landing page expresses this through a committed terracotta opening field, a visible timetable construction grid, one continuous working-day route, and role-specific product scenes.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Warm mineral surfaces with concentrated terracotta and scarce saffron.
- A visible underlying order: timetable grids, connected routes, aligned rails, and quiet geometric seams.
- Bold Manrope typography with one Newsreader italic breath.
- Product truth shown through crisp, information-rich interfaces rather than customer-proof theater.
- Motion that explains connection, state, and sequence, with an equivalent reduced-motion experience.

## Colors

The palette feels sunlit and architectural: parchment and porcelain carry most content, terracotta owns decisive fields and actions, and saffron is reserved for signal and intelligence.

### Primary

- **Foundry Terracotta:** The signature field, primary action, active-navigation, and route color; its deep and soft companions handle interaction and tonal staging.

### Secondary

- **Saffron Signal:** A scarce highlight for intelligence, attention, timeline sparks, and the editorial hero counterpoint.
- **Success Leaf:** Confirmed positive operational states; use its soft companion for status fills.
- **Danger Clay:** Explicit negative or absent states; use its soft companion when the state needs a field.

### Neutral

- **Warm Parchment and Porcelain:** The page canvas and highest-priority working surfaces.
- **Quiet Sand and Quiet Sand Deep:** Secondary panels, interface fragments, segmented controls, and grounded tonal separation.
- **Deep Ink and Body Ink:** Headings, high-contrast fields, interface chrome, and long-form text.
- **Muted Clay:** Supporting copy, metadata, and low-priority labels.
- **Structural Line and Structural Line Strong:** Timetable cells, dividers, rails, and boundaries that communicate actual structure.

**The Concentrated Heat Rule.** Terracotta and saffron occupy committed fields or decisive controls; they are not sprinkled across every card.

**The Semantic Pair Rule.** Leaf, saffron, and danger states always combine color with text, shape, or iconography.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Manrope Variable (with Manrope, Aptos, and Segoe UI fallbacks)

**Body Font:** Manrope Variable (with Manrope, Aptos, and Segoe UI fallbacks)
**Editorial Accent:** Newsreader Variable italic (with Georgia fallback)

**Character:** Manrope gives editorial scale and operational density one disciplined voice. Newsreader appears only as a short human counterpoint, never as the default marketing headline.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (720, fluid 3.9–6rem, 0.9): The first-viewport proposition with a tightly controlled silhouette.
- **Editorial** (430 italic, fluid 2.6–4.6rem, 0.92): One brief emotional continuation immediately after the display statement.
- **Headline** (680, fluid 3.2–6rem, 0.96): Major section theses and transitions.
- **Chapter Title** (650, fluid 1.65–2.65rem, 1.04): Product areas, relay panes, and operational chapters.
- **Title** (750, 18px, 1.2): Cards, foundation items, and dense product headings.
- **Body** (400, 16px, 1.65): Explanatory copy, generally capped near 46–61 characters per line in composed regions.
- **Label** (750, 11px, tracked uppercase): Navigation metadata, status, and interface overlines placed beside or after their headings.
- **Price and Room Code:** Large Manrope numerals with tabular figures; room codes also carry deliberate positive tracking.

**The One Italic Breath Rule.** Editorial italic may soften one phrase in a composition; multiple competing italic moments dilute its effect.

**The Stable Numeral Rule.** Prices, times, dates, generated codes, and changing operational values use tabular numerals so the interface does not jitter.

## Layout

The page uses a fluid section shell capped at 1440px and a visible 12-column construction logic. The opening composition is a 40/60 terracotta-to-parchment split: the offer sits on the left and the diagonal 08:30–18:20 working-day demonstration occupies the right. On wide screens the hero copy is optically lifted rather than vertically drifting, keeping the headline aligned with the dense journey field.

Sections alternate between spacious editorial statements and denser operational proof. Timetable and workflow grids are intentional because their cells communicate time, role, and product structure. They are not decorative bento layouts. The CEO and staff relay preserves web-plus-mobile form factors, while the family product resolves as mobile-only; its desktop row becomes a vertical product relay below 900px. The coming-soon IELTS scene pairs a four-step workflow with a desktop Mock Room preview, then stacks without losing sequence.

At 900px, desktop navigation becomes an opaque mobile sheet, the split hero becomes one column, the day route turns vertical, and multi-pane sections stack. At 640px, the shell becomes 32px narrower than the viewport, buttons become full-width where appropriate, contact/pricing collapse to one column, and the back-to-top control reduces to an icon-only 50px circle. At 380px, the type and role legend compress again. Internal navigation scrolls smoothly without leaving a hash in the URL; reduced motion switches that behavior to immediate movement.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is layered rather than glossy. Tonal fields and structural dividers do most of the work; shadows are reserved for controls, overlapping product panes, floating navigation, devices, the Mock Room stage, and the back-to-top control.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Control:** A one-pixel optical edge with two compact ambient lifts for buttons, icons, and small raised objects.
- **Working Panel:** A one-pixel optical edge plus warm 8px and 3px lifts for product panes and cards that overlap the grid.
- **Hero:** A one-pixel optical edge plus warm 24px and 8px lifts for the dominant device, floating navigation, mobile menu, Mock Room, and return control.

**The Structure Before Shadow Rule.** Use lines for meaningful boundaries and shadows only for true elevation.

## Shapes

The form language is deliberately graduated. Micro interface blocks use 7–12px corners; fields use 14px; strips use 16px; recurring working panels and pricing cards use 18px; compact mobile stages use 20–24px; devices and the clip-reveal menu use 28–32px. Primary actions and segmented states are pills, while role icons and state dots are circles. Nested stages preserve concentric radii.

The four-point Starforge spark is the recurring authored geometry. It appears in the brand mark, route nodes, connectors, and selected product moments with `currentColor`; it is not repeated as a generic icon tile.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** Pill-shaped, at least 44px high; the default conversion control is 52px and large closing actions are 58px.
- **Primary:** Foundry terracotta with porcelain text and the compact ambient control shadow.
- **Porcelain:** A high-contrast signature action on terracotta, optionally with a soft terracotta icon well.
- **Ghost Light:** Transparent on committed dark or terracotta fields with an inset porcelain edge.
- **Hover / Focus / Press:** Hover changes only the relevant color or edge; focus uses a 3px high-contrast outline with 4px offset; press scales to exactly 0.96.

### Chips

- **Style:** Small pill fields in quiet sand, saffron soft, success soft, or danger soft with compact bold labels.
- **State:** Selected and semantic states use text plus field/icon cues; color is never the only distinction.

### Cards / Containers

- **Corner Style:** Operational cards use 16–18px corners, desktop stages use 24px, and devices use 28–32px.
- **Background:** Porcelain on parchment, quiet sand for secondary density, deep ink for executive or desktop chrome, and committed terracotta only for major conversion fields.
- **Shadow Strategy:** Flat or inset-lined by default; Working Panel or Hero shadow only when the object genuinely floats or overlaps.
- **Internal Padding:** 18–30px for full cards and 10–16px for compact product fragments.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** The shipped Mock Room code field uses quiet sand, a 10px corner, bold tabular code text, and a clear adjacent action. The generated-code display uses a saffron-soft field with a dashed signal border.
- **Focus:** Interactive fields and controls inherit the global 3px visible focus ring; focus never relies on color alone.

### Navigation

The desktop header is a floating porcelain pill with a sand segmented navigation track. The current section uses both a terracotta-soft shape and deep terracotta text, while a 2px terracotta progress line grows across the bottom of the bar as the visitor scrolls. It tightens toward the top edge after 90px of scroll.

Mobile navigation is an opaque porcelain sheet revealed with a rounded `clip-path`, small vertical settling, and no background transparency. The menu and close icons swap contextually through opacity, scale, and blur; Escape closes the sheet and returns focus. Internal section links close the menu, move without a persistent URL hash, and keep minimum 44px targets.

### Connected Product Relay

CEO and staff are shown as web-plus-mobile panes connected by a saffron spark route. Family is a clearly phone-shaped, mobile-only pane. On mobile the relay becomes a vertical sequence with the same product order and connection marks.

### Working-Day Journey

One continuous terracotta route links admissions, classroom, staff mobile, finance, and leadership from 08:30 to 18:20. Desktop uses a diagonal route across an explicit timetable grid; mobile uses a vertical continuation line. The route draws once after content is already visible.

### IELTS Mock Room

The coming-soon desktop preview pairs four explicit steps—choose or upload, schedule, generate a random room code, and student entry—with a dark desktop frame and porcelain working surface. It remains visibly labeled as an illustrative workflow and availability is never implied.

### Back to Top

After the page is scrolled, a deep-ink pill appears at the lower right through opacity, scale, and blur. It uses the exact 0.96 press scale, smooth return when motion is allowed, and an icon-only circular form on small screens while retaining its accessible label.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** expose the grid when it explains how people, time, and modules connect.
- **Do** keep the confirmed brand promise directly below the hero heading before explanatory copy.
- **Do** preserve CEO and staff as web plus mobile, family as mobile only, and IELTS desktop tools as coming soon.
- **Do** preserve tabular numerals for prices, times, dates, codes, and changing operational values.
- **Do** use exact 0.96 press feedback, explicit transition properties, hash-free internal navigation, and reduced-motion equivalents.
- **Do** keep interactive targets at least 44px with visible keyboard focus.

### Don't:

- **Don't** turn the page into a uniform bento grid of interchangeable cards; timetable cells are reserved for real time and product structure.
- **Don't** use generic neon gradients, glassmorphism, or floating icon tiles as substitutes for product proof.
- **Don't** fabricate customer counts, testimonials, savings, rankings, availability dates, billing terms, or plan entitlements.
- **Don't** imply a family web product or currently available IELTS desktop workflow.
- **Don't** animate layout width, use `transition: all`, hide essential content behind motion, or make motion the sole state cue.
